# Call Prep: Emily Brierley, Head of MBA Admissions

_Friday 24 July 2026, 11:30 (Buenos Aires time)_

## Goal of the call

Leave with: (1) a firm date for college membership, (2) real clarity on how the college
queue works, and (3) if the conversation allows, play the final card: visa cost
protection and a contingency plan. Tone: calm, firm, solution-focused. Not here to vent
or accuse, here to secure commitments.

## Opening (about 30 seconds)

> "Thank you for making the time. Simply put: I was admitted in round 4 and I am still
> without college membership after four rejections, while my partner, same profile and
> same joint application, was placed. Accommodation is not the issue, I arranged that
> privately. The only thing missing is a membership so my CAS can be issued and I can
> apply for my visa, and that single piece is holding up my whole process and starting
> to cost me money, with my flight booked for 25 August."

## Key facts (ready to cite)

- Admitted in round 4. College choices submitted 1 June.
- Four rejections: Wolfson, Hughes Hall, St Edmund's (after a reconsideration request),
  Homerton (capacity, 21 July). Clare Hall was full.
- Homerton turnaround was about a week (sent 15 July, result 21 July). Now with
  Robinson; Elizabeth (24 July) recommends Lucy Cavendish next if Robinson declines.
- My partner was placed at St Edmund's; same nationality and profile, applied jointly.
  Her CAS was issued the Monday after her Friday acceptance, with the other nine
  conditions already met.
- Elizabeth (24 July): my details go to ISO as soon as a college confirms, but the CAS
  "is not immediate as it also has to go through ISO".
- Membership is described as guaranteed, normally allocated 2 to 3 weeks after accepting
  the offer.
- The CAS requires all 10 conditions; membership is the only one outside my control (my
  instalment is paid).
- Visa: flight 25 August. Standard £558 (about 15 working days); Priority £1,058
  (+£500); Super Priority £1,558 (+£1,000). A late CAS forces a £500 to £1,000 upgrade,
  roughly one month's rent, plus the risk of not having the visa in time.

## Core arguments

**1. Membership is guaranteed.** Being left without one after two months is a process
failure against a promise the programme made, not me failing a bar.

**2. The University sponsors the visa, not the college.** The CAS depends on a confirmed
membership, not a specific college. I am not asking anyone to override a college's
judgment, I am asking the school to ensure I am placed somewhere with capacity before my
visa window closes.

**3. I only need membership, not accommodation.** Arranged privately. This removes the
couple-flat bottleneck and widens the pool of colleges that can take me.

**4. Fairness: payment versus delivery.** I was required to commit and pay my
instalments in full and on time, yet the one crucial deliverable that only the school
controls, the path to the CAS, is still outstanding, and its timing is highly delicate.
It should not be possible to be accepted and made to pay first, then be blocked on a
component the school owns.

**5. A firm date.**
> "What is a realistic date by which I will have membership? Is there an internal
> deadline to finalise all memberships for the cohort?"

**6. Timing and queue inconsistency (ask as a genuine question).**
> "I was told in writing that Sidney Sussex closed on 17 July and could not be pursued,
> yet cohort members were admitted to Sidney and Hughes in the last few days, some round
> 5. I want to understand the queue. When I asked about Sidney before the 17th, why was
> my application sent to Homerton instead? And can I still be considered at Sidney or
> Hughes?"

**7. Criteria (neutral, not an accusation).**
> "My partner and I share nationality, background and profile and applied together, yet
> she was placed and I was not. To understand it: is college gender balance a factor
> that is weighed in allocation?"

## The final card: visa protection and contingency (play last)

- **Oxford precedent.** "Oxford, also a collegiate system, reportedly issues the CAS
  immediately after MBA admission rather than waiting on college placement. So a
  collegiate model can decouple the CAS from a specific college. Is a comparable
  solution, or any exception, possible here?"
- **CAS speed.** "I understand the CAS goes through ISO and is not immediate. But
  Luciana's CAS was issued the Monday after her Friday acceptance. All my other
  conditions are met, so I am asking that mine be treated with the same speed the moment
  a college confirms, and that Admissions actively pushes it through ISO given the delay
  so far has been on the school's side."
- **Cost protection.** "If this delay forces me onto a priority or super-priority visa,
  an extra £500 to £1,000, roughly one month's rent, will the school cover or contribute
  to that cost?"
- **Contingency.** "If no college is secured by [date], what is the plan? I need to
  resolve this for this cohort. I turned down three other offers, gave up my income and
  booked flights, so deferring is not something I can absorb."
