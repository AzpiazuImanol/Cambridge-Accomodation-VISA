# Honest take — is this normal, and what actually moves it

_My candid read of the situation, plus what the research says. Sources at the bottom._

## Short version

1. **The nationality theory (Argentinians rejected while Indians get in) is almost
   certainly wrong** — and putting it in the email will hurt you.
2. **What's really happening is timing + capacity**, and it's a known, common
   bottleneck at Cambridge — but it is legitimately urgent because it's now gating
   your visa.
3. **Your strongest facts are that college membership is _guaranteed_ for MBA
   students, and that the _University_ (not the college) sponsors the visa and issues
   the CAS.** Those are the levers to push.

## Is what's happening to us normal?

Partly yes, partly no — and the distinction matters.

**The normal process** is that MBA students are **guaranteed college membership**.
You accept your offer, and roughly **2–3 weeks later you are _allocated_ a college** —
you don't normally shop college‑by‑college and collect rejections. Not every college
takes MBA students every year, and you don't find out which one until nearer the time.

**What's unusual in our case** is being individually turned down by one college after
another (Wolfson → Hughes → Clare Hall full → St Edmund's). That pattern means we're
in the **late / overflow allocation path**: by the time we're in the queue, the
colleges that take MBAs — especially those with **couple/family accommodation**, which
is scarcer — have already filled their quota for the year, so placement is being done
manually, college by college. That is a **calendar-and-capacity problem**, not a
verdict on us.

## Is it because we're Argentinian? (my honest answer: almost certainly not)

I understand the frustration, but I'd drop this framing, for three reasons:

- **Colleges allocate on capacity and category** (single vs couple, accommodation
  needs, timing), not nationality. A couple needing couple accommodation late in the
  cycle is the hard case to place — regardless of passport.
- **The over-representation of Indian students reflects applicant-pool volume**, not a
  quota against South Americans. More applicants from a country → more admits from it.
- **Cambridge colleges are heavily scrutinised on equality.** A nationality allegation,
  made to Elizabeth (who is the person actually helping us), would put her on the
  defensive and **weaken the ask**. We want her fighting for us, not defending the
  institution.

So in the email we can absolutely say the repeated rejections are **hard to understand
and stressful** — that's fair and firm — **without alleging discrimination**.

## The levers that actually matter

- **Membership is guaranteed.** Cambridge Judge's own materials say MBA students are
  *guaranteed* College membership. So being left without one this late isn't us failing
  a bar — it's a process delay against a promise the school made. That's fair pressure.
- **The University sponsors the visa, not the college.** The University holds the
  student sponsor licence; the **Postgraduate Admissions Office issues the CAS**. UKVI
  treats the colleges' premises as part of the University's premises. So the CAS depends
  on *a* confirmed membership — **not on a specific college.**
- **Membership is the last open CAS condition.** The CAS issues once **all ten offer
  conditions** are met. We're clearing the rest ourselves (commitment fee paid), so the
  clean ask is: can the CAS issue with membership still pending, or — failing that — what
  gives us a firm date / accelerates a placement.
- **Keep accommodation out of it (Lu's call, and correct).** The CAS does **not** require
  us to have housing secured, and housing isn't Elizabeth's responsibility. Raising it
  only adds noise she has to read past. Private accommodation stays a separate workstream.

## The visa math (why the deadline is real) — fact-checked

- Flights: **25 August**.
- You need the **CAS first**, then apply for the visa from Argentina.
- UKVI Student visa fees (applications from outside the UK, schedule effective **8 April
  2026**):

  | Service | Total cost | Decision time |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Standard | **£558** | ~3 weeks / 15 working days |
  | Priority ("express") | **£1,058** (£558 + £500) | 5 working days |
  | Super Priority | **£1,558** (£558 + £1,000) | next working day |

  Separately, the **Immigration Health Surcharge is £776/year**, paid upfront regardless
  of speed.
- **So "express roughly doubles it" is accurate:** £558 → £1,058 = **~1.9×**. If we're
  late enough to need **Super Priority** (next-day), it's £1,558 ≈ **~2.8×** — worse.
  Either way, a late CAS forces a paid upgrade of **£500–£1,000** we'd otherwise avoid.
- If membership lands in **early–mid August**, we're forced onto priority/super‑priority
  to make the flights — an avoidable extra cost, and a real risk of not having the visa in
  hand in time. **That** is the concrete, legitimate pressure point for the email.
- **On the early flight:** we're arriving before registration on purpose (family visit +
  rising fares), booked before we understood the CAS needed all ten conditions. Say this
  in the email — otherwise the natural reply is "you don't need to arrive that early," which
  dodges the real point (the visa still has to be processed *before* we fly, whenever we go).

## Bottom line

This is recoverable and fairly common, but it needs **escalation now**, not more
waiting. Ask Elizabeth for: a firm membership date under the guarantee; whether the CAS
can progress now that we've arranged our own housing; and the **names/emails** of the
MBA Programme Office / college liaison / Postgraduate Admissions (CAS) team so we can
push from our side.
