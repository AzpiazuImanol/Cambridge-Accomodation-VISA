1. Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS)

The CAS is the first step and gates everything else — you need it before you can apply for the visa.

  • The CAS is issued by the University to support the visa application, and will be issued from April.
  • Your CAS is issued by the International Student Office once the MBA office confirms that you have met all offer conditions (there are 10 offer conditions — we know them; college membership is one of them).
  • You must enter the CAS reference number in your student visa application form. In the form you only enter that reference number — no additional CAS documentation is required, just the number.
  • Dual nationals: you must tell the MBA office which passport you will use to apply for your visa. The passport used for the CAS must be the same one used for the visa application.

2. The visa application process

While the CAS has to be resolved first, several things can be progressed in parallel:

  • Meet all offer conditions as soon as possible.
  • Apply for a TB (tuberculosis) certificate (if applicable). Whether it is required depends mainly on where you have been resident over the last 6 months, rather than on your nationality. The list of countries where it is required is published online (official source below).
  • Ensure you meet the financial requirements (covering course fees plus living costs).
  • Part of the process varies depending on whether or not you can use the ID (identity) app provided by UKVI vs. enrolling biometrics in person.

Once the visa requirements are satisfied — CAS obtained, financial information attached, and the TB requirement met where applicable — you apply online at gov.uk/student-visa/apply.

The form asks about yourself, your travel history, your personal circumstances and the course you will take at Cambridge. There is a step-by-step guide on the ISO website; it is recommended to review it before and during the application, and it includes a useful FAQ on the form's questions.

Fees to pay when applying

  • Immigration Health Surcharge (IHS): £1,164. This is roughly £776 per year, pro-rated across the ~1.5-year course length. Paying the IHS gives access to UK healthcare at no additional cost.
  • Visa application fee: £558.

3. Supporting documents

Exact requirements depend on nationality. The core list is:

  • Passport
  • TB certificate (if applicable)
  • Financial documents (course fees and living costs)
  • Possible other documents
  • Official translation if documents aren't in English

Process: submit documentation online before attending a biometric appointment. Then go to the Visa Application Centre to enrol biometrics; you may also have a credibility interview.

It is not unusual to be asked for additional information. If that happens, the ISO invites you to contact them for clarification on the document types needed. Any document not in English needs an official translation that meets the immigration rules' requirements.

4. Financial requirements — course fees & living costs

You must demonstrate the ability to cover course fees + living costs. The course-fee figure is set by your CAS.

  • Living costs: £10,539 (9 × £1,171 per month).
  • Course fees: as stated on the CAS.

Three ways to evidence the funds

  1. Cash funds in the bank
    • The evidence must show the funds have been held for 28 days. That period must fall within the 31 days before you submit your application. The 28 days is counted from the date of the last transaction on the most recent bank statement.
    • Can be evidenced with a bank statement, letter or certificate of deposit.
    • Only immediately-accessible / liquid cash counts. Investments such as shares/stocks, pension withdrawals, crypto (e.g. Bitcoin), etc. are NOT acceptable forms of evidence.
    • You can use money from a parent or partner, BUT: using a parent's funds also requires your original birth certificate and a letter of consent from the parent(s); if the funds belong to a partner, they must either be present in the UK or applying for their visa at the same time as you.
    • If you use evidence held in a bank, UKVI may contact the bank to verify it.
  2. Educational loan from a regulated financial institution — evidence with the loan letter.
  3. Official financial sponsorship (government, international scholarship agency, international organisation, or the University) — a letter confirming the sponsorship details as evidence.

Deductions — what reduces the amount you must evidence

  • Course fees paid in advance are stated on the CAS and deducted from the fees amount you must demonstrate.
  • College accommodation fees paid in advance, up to £1,529, can be stated on the CAS and deducted from the living-costs amount.
  • Scholarships from the University or College can be included on the CAS and reduce the financial evidence required. This applies only to University/College scholarships, not to third-party scholarships.

5. Differentiation nationals (low-risk countries)

Nationals of certain countries do not need to submit financial evidence with their application. The full list shown was:

Australia, Austria, Bahrain, Barbados, Belgium, Botswana, Brazil, Brunei, Bulgaria, Cambodia, Canada, Chile, China, Croatia, Republic of Cyprus, Czech Republic, Denmark, The Dominican Republic, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hong Kong, Hungary, Iceland, Indonesia, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Kazakhstan, Kuwait, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Macao, Malaysia, Malta, Mauritius, Mexico, Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Oman, Peru, Poland, Portugal, Qatar, Romania, Serbia, Singapore, Slovakia, Slovenia, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand, Tunisia, United Arab Emirates, United States of America.

What this means:

  • You do not need to provide any financial evidence with your application.
  • You must still hold the required funds in the manner specified — UKVI reserves the right to request the evidence during the application process.

Note for us: Italy and Spain are on the list; Argentina is not. Lucy stressed it is still important to have the evidence ready and to know the requirements, even as a differentiation national — you simply won't be asked to provide it unless specifically requested.

6. Biometrics & Visa Application Centre

  • Submit documents online first, then attend a biometric enrolment appointment.
  • How biometrics work varies by country — whether you can use the UKVI ID app or must attend a Visa Application Centre in person (for Argentina → VFS Global).
  • You may also be asked to attend a credibility interview.

7. Arrival in Cambridge (visa perspective)

  • Your visa is granted as a digital status (e-Visa) — nothing is placed in your passport. You'll receive instructions by email on how to access it.
  • Check in advance that the e-Visa is correctly linked to your passport.
  • The e-Visa states the date from which the visa is valid — 1 month before the course start date, at the earliest (≈ 10 August in our case).
  • You cannot enter the UK as a student earlier than your visa start date.
  • You can check online once the visa has been granted.
  • Do NOT apply for a Schengen visa once you arrive in the UK — you'd have to send your passport to an embassy, but your passport is needed for course registration. If you need a Schengen visa, wait until after MBA registration.

What this means for us (Imanol & Luciana)

Practical takeaways and open action items, derived from the rules above.

  1. Which passport to use (dual nationals) → the EU one. Apply with the Italian passport (Imanol) and the Spanish passport (Luciana): both are differentiation nationals, so we would not have to submit financial evidence. The Argentine passport is not on the list. Use the same passport for the CAS and the visa, and tell the MBA office which one.
  2. TB test → most likely not required. It's based on residence (last 6 months), and Argentina is not on the UKVI list (the South American countries that are: Bolivia, Ecuador, Guyana, Paraguay, Peru). Confirm on the official page before ruling it out.
  3. Financial evidence — reduced, not eliminated. As differentiation nationals we won't need to submit evidence, but we must still hold the funds in the manner specified, and UKVI can request them.
  4. Funding via the Morgan Stanley account — the key thing to sort now. Two rules to watch: (a) investments/securities are NOT acceptable — funds must be liquid cash with immediate access; (b) the money must sit in the account for 28 days before applying. Action: as soon as the account is open and funded as cash, start the 28-day clock, and consider contacting the ISO to explain that this will be the account used to fund the MBA and to ask how the 28-day rule applies to our timing. Being differentiation nationals softens the risk (no submission required) but does not remove the underlying requirement.
  5. Deductions to remember. Prepaid course fees, prepaid College accommodation (up to £1,529), and any University/College scholarships are stated on the CAS and reduce the amount we must evidence.
  6. Arrival timing looks fine. Visa valid from ~1 month before course start (≈ 10 August); our 25 August flight is after that. We cannot enter the UK as students before the visa start date. Don't apply for any Schengen visa until after registration (passport needed).
